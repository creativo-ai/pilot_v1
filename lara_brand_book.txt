Brand Name: Lara's Flowers
Industry/Field: Boutique Floral Studio

About Us & Mission:
We specialize in bespoke, sustainable floral arrangements. I started this whole journey because I desperately want to bring a slice of untamed, wild beauty into people's busy urban lives. Looking ahead, I really want us to grow into the city's most trusted eco-conscious florist, known for making every single recipient feel deeply cherished and connected to nature.

Core Values & USP:
We believe heavily in sustainability, artistic freedom, and supporting our community. Unlike the big wire services or local chain shops like 1-800-Flowers or BloomNation who use generic imported stems, we exclusively forage and source from local micro-farms within a 50-mile radius. That local, wild-gathered approach is our absolute magic. 

Target Audience:
Our main clients are usually professional women, maybe in their late 20s to mid-50s, living right here in the metro area. They have a bit of disposable income and care deeply about sustainable living, interior design, and home aesthetics. Their biggest struggle is finding gifts that feel genuinely personal rather than mass-produced. They are busy, so they prefer quick, visual communication—like an Instagram DM or a text—rather than long, formal emails. 

Brand Voice & Messaging:
When we talk to our community, we try to keep things poetic, gentle, and passionately knowledgeable. I never want us to use words like "cheap", "discounted", or "standardized"—those are completely banned from our vocabulary. We just want people to realize that every stem tells a story, and that locally grown blooms actually last longer on your table. Oh, and our little motto we use on our cards is: "Wild beauty, locally grown."

Visual Identity (For the designers):
I'm leaning into a soft petal pink (hex code #F2C4CE) as our main color, grounded by a deep forest green (#2C5E3B). We use the "Cormorant Garamond" font for our elegant headers, and "Lato" for the standard reading text. We also have a minimalist line-art logo of a fern that we only use in black and white on our wrapping paper.
